# The Archivist's Handbook: Content & Editorial Rules

This document governs the editorial standards, narrative logic, and content layout for the Fictional History Archive. All articles submitted to the database must comply with these protocols.

## 1. Core Principles

1. **Fictional Origin:** The underlying source text must be a work of fiction, fully detached from actual historical events[cite: 5]. Satirical, parodic, or intentionally historically inaccurate adaptations are welcome[cite: 6].
2. **Journalistic Tone:** All entries must look, sound, and read like a credible news broadcast or newspaper report[cite: 9, 50]. Casual narration, conversational framing, first-person blogging, or speculative fan theories are strictly forbidden[cite: 214, 288].
3. **Narrative Impact:** Focus on massive, culturally shifting events that carry substantial narrative weight in their native lore (e.g., galactic wars, cataclysmic disruptions, fallen empires)[cite: 11, 12, 52]. Avoid minor trivia or peripheral plot beats[cite: 11, 52].

## 2. Timeline Classification Rules

To catalog timelines without causing cross-contamination, assign entries to one of two primary spaces[cite: 31]:

- **`On Earth`**: Covers events occurring on planet Earth, including alternative-history variants or near-future projections[cite: 28, 30, 63].
- **`Not On Earth`**: Covers deep space, extraterrestrial colony worlds, alternative planetary systems, or strictly fantasy realms[cite: 29, 64].

_Note: Temporal paradoxes are explicitly treated as irrelevant; the localized internal logic of the specific fictional world takes complete precedence over overarching timelines[cite: 24, 40]._

## 3. Special Formatting Features

### Feature A: Multiversal Perspectives

When separate fictional universes address the exact same real-world historical event or date (e.g., the different accounts of November 22, 1963, across King's _11/22/63_, _Quantum Leap_, and _Red Dwarf_), the frontend UI must generate a tabbed display engine[cite: 89, 108].

- The system groups individual files utilizing a matching `multiverse_id`.
- The reader must be allowed to toggle seamlessly across the different parallel historical records on a single page, contrasting their distinct timeline behaviors[cite: 143, 144].

### Feature B: Time Traveler Case Chronicles

Characters who actively manipulate time or move across centuries must not be cataloged with static standard timelines. Instead, layout configurations must present them as **"Active Sighting & Missing Persons Reports"**[cite: 37, 68].

- Archivists must report these encounters as alleged, unverified chronological sightings logged across varying dates and locations[cite: 38, 69].

## 4. Mandatory Citation Framework

No historical entry can exist without absolute source provenance[cite: 15]. Every single layout page must compile an unalterable **Source Citation Block** at the bottom footer containing[cite: 15, 220]:

- Fictional Work Title [cite: 16, 231]
- Medium Typology & Primary Creator (e.g., Book by Stephen King) [cite: 101, 233]
- Real-world Release/Publication Date [cite: 20, 57]
- Structural Context Statement summarizing why this moment broke or reshaped its native universe[cite: 12, 86].
